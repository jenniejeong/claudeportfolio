01

Overview

Problem

A growing nonprofit’s WordPress site couldn’t explain its model, show donors their impact, or surface Fellows’ stories, and the team had no technical staff to maintain a rebuild.

Solution

Restructured the navigation and information architecture, and redesigned the FAQ, post-donation hub, and testimonials page, all built as no-code components the client can run on their own.

Results

Shipped August 2026 and live today. Donor-campaign visitors show an engagement rate above 80%, and the client now updates the site independently.

ConnectED Fellows supports local education leaders across Guatemala, Nicaragua, and Colombia. As a Product Designer on a Develop for Good team, I helped redesign the site’s core user flows, homepage narrative, and donation experience so non-technical stakeholders could connect with prospective donors and partners.

I owned three pages end to end, from wireframe to final design: the FAQ, the post-donation engagement hub, and the testimonials page.

02

Context & Problem Space

ConnectED Fellows is a nonprofit that equips local educators in rural Latin America to lead community-driven educational projects. With its expansion into Colombia, the network now reaches 92 schools and over 19,000 people.

92schools in the network
19,000+people reached
3countries: Guatemala, Nicaragua, Colombia

Despite that growing impact, the website, the primary touchpoint for donors, corporate partners, and prospective fellows, was underperforming. It failed to convert casual visitors into active supporters because of four design friction points:

  • Dense, academic language

    The site relied on academic terminology instead of clear, relatable storytelling, so visitors struggled to grasp what the organization actually does.

  • Opaque value proposition & model

    The 2-year fellowship model and its 7 phases were buried across subpages instead of introduced on the homepage.

  • Unclear donation impact

    Giving felt transactional. Donors couldn’t picture how a $50 gift versus a $250 gift created change in rural classrooms.

  • Dead-end post-donation flow

    After donating, the experience ended abruptly, with no path to stay involved.

Constraint

The client had no technical staff, so every solution had to be modular and easy to manage in WordPress without custom code.

03

Research & Strategic Design Goals

Each designer audited the existing site to find where information felt unclear, and we combined those findings with the questions the client hears most often. From that, we set three design objectives:

01Clarity & Storytelling

Restructure the information architecture so any visitor understands the mission, model, and geographic reach within 10 seconds of landing.

02Transparent Giving

Design an impact-linked donation experience with clear preset tiers and flexible payment options.

03Sustained Engagement

Turn the post-donation moment into a hub that converts one-time donors into long-term community members.

04

Design Execution & Core Flows

4.1Restructuring IA & narrative flow

  • Above-the-fold clarity

    Redesigned the hero section and primary navigation to answer three questions: What is ConnectED? Why does it exist? How does the model work?

  • Visualizing the 7-phase model

    Designed a visual breakdown of the fellowship lifecycle on the homepage so visitors can scan the process before diving deeper.

  • Embedded accordion FAQ

    Instead of a standalone FAQ page, I placed a collapsible FAQ directly in the browsing flow, right after visitors learn about the model and impact, which is when questions about getting involved come up. I started with a wireframe to align with the client on structure, then explored three color treatments in ConnectED’s orange-and-white branding before finalizing.

4.2An empathetic donation experience

  • Impact-linked tiers

    Structured giving around $50, $100, $150, $200, and $250 tiers, each paired with what that amount funds.

  • Frictionless modal integration

    Designed layout support for an Every.org donation modal offering Apple Pay, Venmo, credit, and bank transfer while keeping the brand consistent.

4.3Post-donation engagement hub

I replaced the generic confirmation screen with a hub built around four goals: transparency about how donations are used, donor trust, continued involvement, and staying connected. It brings together newsletter signup, paths to partners, fellows, and the team, Charity Navigator certification, volunteer options, and social sharing, with copy written to feel like a welcome into a community rather than a receipt.

4.4Interactive testimonials

  • Explore by what matters to you

    I turned a static wall of quotes into a filterable page with categories like Leadership Journey, Community Mobilization, Student Impact, and Women in Leadership, so partners and prospective fellows can each find the stories that speak to them.

  • Add your story

    I added a submission path so fellows, donors, and partners can share their own experiences, which keeps the page fresh without extra work for staff.

4.5Bilingual newsletter & analytics (team build)

  • Language-based routing

    Newsletter signups route to separate English and Spanish lists with double opt-in, so Spanish-speaking fellows receive content in their own language.

  • GA4 tracking & dashboard

    We defined a tracking plan for key actions (donation clicks, newsletter signups, volunteer and partner clicks, report downloads) and built a dashboard that breaks down traffic and engagement by channel, including WhatsApp and Facebook.

05

Design System & Constraints

Primary Blue#2C4450
Accent Orange#EE8721
  • Mobile-first responsiveness

    Optimized touch targets, modals, and navigation for mobile, since many visitors arrive from social media and WhatsApp links.

  • Modular maintainability

    Built reusable Elementor components in Primary Blue and Accent Orange so client staff can update copy and fellow stories on their own.

  • No added cost

    Built almost entirely on tools the client already paid for (WordPress, WPForms, Elementor add-ons) plus free tiers, so the redesign added essentially no new software cost.

06

Key Success Metrics

Before this project, the team had no visibility into who was visiting or what was working. We built a tracking plan for the actions that matter and turned it into a dashboard the team can read on their own.
